Help your child get enough iron

Iron is the nutrient young children most often fall short on, and low iron can lead to anaemia. Tell us a little about your household and we will show how your situation compares with other children, plus foods that help. This is general guidance, not a diagnosis.

How the comparison works
The comparison comes from a statistical model fitted on 664 US children in NHANES 2021–2023 (340 with a blood test). It looks at household circumstances, not at what your child eats, so it cannot tell you whether your child is low in iron. Only a blood test can do that: ask your child's doctor if you are worried, or if your child seems unusually tired, pale or irritable.
